### Why is AI Essential?
Too much data leads to limits in human reasoning.

* **Information Overload:** Occurs when individuals face more data or choices than they can process effectively. 
* **Decision Fatigue:** Occurs when making many choices in a short period drains mental energy.
* **Inconsistent Judgment:** Means people do not always make the same decision in similar situations.

*Hence, humans develop tools to tackle these issues.*

---

### Responding to the Data Challenge
Even with traditional tools, decision-making remains a challenge due to the nature of data. 

**Raw data is often:**
* Missing context or the entire picture
* Subject to uncertain outcomes (even with large volumes of data)
* Inconsistently structured

*AI can solve these challenges with minimal human intervention.*

---

### Patterns, Insights, and Automation
AI can scan, sort, and connect data at scale. It identifies patterns, offers insights about potential outcomes, and automates tasks that would otherwise require significant time or effort.

* **Pattern Recognition:** Makes sense of large and messy datasets by identifying hidden relationships. 
* **Insights:** Estimates likely outcomes based on past data (e.g., recommendation systems).
* **Automation:** Processes large volumes of data in various formats. 
  > **Example:** Invoice processing tools use AI to extract key details from PDFs, scanned images, or emails—even when layouts vary—solving the issue of inconsistent data structures.

## Section 2: Capabilities & Ethics of AI

AI systems are **not** conscious, sentient (the ability to experience emotions, be aware, and have experiences), or self-aware. AI doesn't have emotions; it only generates replies based on what it has learned from data.

### Core Capabilities
* **Powerful Language Capabilities:** Understanding and generating text, speech, and translations across many languages.
* **Perception Capabilities:** Interpreting images and video.
* **Decision-Making Capabilities:** Forecasting, automation, and recommendations.

### Limitations
* **Bias:** Leading toward certain ideas more than others, even if the training data is balanced.
* **Hallucination:** Generating outcomes that are believable but false.
* **Gaps in Training Data:** Missing or incomplete information in the dataset.
* **Misunderstood AI Output:** Happens when people misinterpret what an AI system produces, often as a result of blindly believing the output.

---

### AI Ethics
Ethics is a set of moral principles that help people discern between right and wrong. **AI ethics** is a multidisciplinary field that examines how to maximize the beneficial impact of AI while minimizing risks and adverse outcomes.

#### IBM's Approach to AI Ethics
Five pillars that aim to promote AI that is clear, safe, and respectful to people:

1. **Explainability:** AI should be easy to understand. When it makes a choice, people should be able to explain how it arrived at that decision.
2. **Fairness:** AI should treat everyone fairly. It shouldn’t give better results to one group over another.
3. **Robustness:** AI-powered systems must be actively defended from deliberate attempts to manipulate them (e.g., prompt attacks targeted against specific groups).
4. **Transparency:** People should know how AI works and when it is being used.
5. **Privacy:** AI must protect personal information.

### How AI Learns from Us
AI learns from your browsing history, social media interactions, and other activities (**digital footprint**). It actively improves based on user inputs and demands, allowing it to provide better answers to future queries.

---

### How AI Tools Use Our Data
**Machine Learning (ML)** is a type of artificial intelligence where computer algorithms learn from data.

#### Learning Workflow Process
1. **Collect Data:** Record user actions and digital footprints.
2. **Identify Patterns:** Infer conclusions by correlating features within the data.
3. **Generate Output:** Provide output based on data inference.
4. **Receive Feedback:** Evaluate user responses and feedback on the output.
5. **Update the Model:** Refine the model based on the feedback received.

### Making Informed Decisions
We must understand how our interactions influence AI outcomes while staying aware of AI's strengths and limitations.

* **Consent and Permissions:** Choices you make about what information you allow an AI tool to access or use. Understanding agreements helps you stay aware of what control and rights you give up versus retain.
* **Data Privacy Practices:** Rules and methods organizations employ to protect your information. Covers how data is collected, securely stored, and shared.
* **Responsible AI Use:** Making choices that are fair, legal, and respectful when interacting with AI tools.

### Ambient vs. Embedded AI
* **Ambient AI:** Manages surroundings quietly in the background (e.g., smart thermostats adjusting lighting and temperature).
* **Embedded AI:** Powers devices directly from within (e.g., smartphones, self-driving cars).

---

### Design Behind AI-Powered Tools
Complex systems trained to recognize patterns, process data, and generate helpful responses in real time.

#### Personal vs. Business Use
* **Individuals:** Used for personal tasks and productivity.
* **Businesses:** Enterprise AI tools solve complex business problems and aid executive decision-making (e.g., IBM's Granite and Watsonx).
